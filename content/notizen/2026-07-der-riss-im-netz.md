## A burning heart

A heart does not burn. It pumps, it tires, it stops — fire is not among the things that happen to it. And yet everyone who reads the sentence "her heart burned" understands at once what is meant, and feels more in it than any literal description could deliver. Between the organ and the flame there is no connection in the world. The connection arises only in the sentence — and holds nevertheless. This force has a place.

## The sign without ground

At the beginning of the twentieth century the linguist Ferdinand de Saussure laid open a basic fact of language that has not been reversible since: the sign — sound pattern and concept, *signifiant* and *signifié* — is not joined by any natural relation between the two. It is joined because a language community holds it so. There is no reason why the sound sequence "heart" should mean this organ and not some other word. **The connection between sign and signified is arbitrary.**

That is a description at first, not a permission. But it has a consequence that reaches beyond linguistics: what is arbitrarily connected is also movable. The sign does not hang from a fixed anchoring in the signified — it hangs from other signs, from its place in a net of differences. "Heart" means what it means partly because it is not "liver" and not "stone". The net carries the sense. Not the thing.

## Metaphor as displacement

Exactly here the metaphor sets in. It is no violation of a rule, no slip of meaning. It is the exploitation of the gap that arbitrariness itself tears open. "Her heart burned" takes two regions that were not adjacent in ordinary usage — organ and flame — and forces them into a neighborhood that did not exist before. The sense arises not although heart and fire are strangers, but *because* they are: the distance is the place where the metaphor holds fast something a literal description — quickened pulse, agitation, excess — could only list, not bind. The metaphor shifts the net to make sayable what was not sayable at its old place.

## What the substrate knows of the net

The *Sprachsubstrat* — the language substrate: what a language model supplies in statistically probable formulation — knows this net with a density no single person ever reaches. It has fully charted the neighborhoods that billions of texts have drawn between concepts: concepts become places, their relations become directions between those places. That is why it can supply "a burning heart" the moment the context calls for it — the neighborhood has long been drawn in the corpus, centuries of love poetry have repeated it. This is no small achievement. It is almost the metaphor.

## The tear

Almost — and exactly there lies the border. <u>The substrate charts the neighborhoods that already exist in the net. The metaphor sets one that did not yet exist there.</u> The difference is not that the one connection is improbable and the other probable — a prompt that asks for it will get the remote connection from the substrate too, since remoteness is relative to the context it is currently serving. The difference lies elsewhere: charting maps the space as it is. A new metaphor extends it.

For that it needs an outside — not in the sense of a fact to be verified (that is the record axis, a different question), but in the sense of a point of reference the corpus had not provided for: an experience, a pain, an urgency that motivates the displacement before it is spoken. Whoever knows only the net can find every place in it. **The tear that forces two hitherto strange places together is set only by someone standing outside the net.**

## What is missing in the Geist

The diagnostic formula this project runs under is *Geist aus der Maschine* — how much of a text's visible Geist was imported from the substrate. In the metaphor, what that formula means shows more sharply than anywhere else. What is missing when the substrate writes alone shows least where facts are missing. It shows where the tears are missing — where a text only repeats the neighborhoods the net already knew, and draws no new one. The seal records how much of the linguistic surface and how much of the ideas come from the charting — and thereby leaves visible where a tear would have to be sought that came from outside.
