## 1. Core position

**The pragmatic level.** The question of authorship is not settled on the level where it is sought. It is sought in the wording and the content — what a text consists of and what it means. It is settled one level above: in who does what with a text and what they stand behind. Two texts can agree word for word and differ in everything that follows from them — whether anyone can be questioned, whether anyone retracts, whether anyone carries the consequence. Naming this level does not add a fifth foundation to the linguistic, legal, speech-act, and anthropological layers of this document. It orders them: it states which level is load-bearing.

The shift has a historical occasion. Wording and meaning were always scarce; whoever could produce correct, idiomatic, clearly meant sentences held something not everyone held. That scarcity is gone — such language is now available at no marginal cost, in any quantity, in any register. What is not supplied along with it is the party who means something, addresses someone, retracts, and bears the consequence. Where one level becomes freely available, the weight moves to the next.

On this level, what the substrate lacks is not understanding but commitment. It enters into no commitment. It asserts nothing, because to assert is to expose oneself to accountability, and nothing that has nothing to lose can do so. What accrues without this act is not speech but material — language awaiting an act that is not its own. **This formulation is deliberately not a claim about the interior of the model.** It holds whether or not language models turn out to possess semantics, world models, or internal representations: a model that understood perfectly would still be a model that asserts nothing. The anti-anthropomorphism of this document therefore takes the form *it enters into no commitment*, not *it has no will*.

A language model is accordingly not an artificial intelligence in the anthropomorphic sense. It operates on the surface of language — what Saussure called the *signifiant* — and in Benveniste's terms it produces *énoncés* without performing *énonciation*, which is the same finding stated on the linguistic level: what is absent is the act, not the meaning. It is a statistically condensed echo of the entire written corpus, made available for re-actualization. That it has no intentionality, no standpoint, no will to utterance is the vivid form of this position and stays serviceable as illustration; it is no longer what carries the position.

This is not a philosophical preference. It is a description of a relation, and it is testable in the only way that bears on the institution: by the practical difference it makes. Where no difference follows in what can be asked, retracted, or answered for, none is claimed. The institution of Maschinenschrift is built on this description and rejects all framings that smuggle a second subject into the writing relation.

The model is therefore not a collaborator, assistant, or co-author. It is a **language substrate** *(Sprachsubstrat)*: a medium through which the author writes. The relation is closer to that of a writer to a printing press than that of a writer to an editor. The word carries the position more exactly than its choosing knew: a substrate is that *on which* someone else works — a ground, not an action.

The name **Maschinenschrift** carries this position by historical inheritance. It denoted, in the twentieth century, the product of the typewriter — text that had passed through a technical apparatus, written by a human, mediated by a machine. The registry extends this lineage. It does not name a new phenomenon. It names the next chapter of an old one.

**The diagnostic formula.** The project's working diagnosis of what changes with the substrate is condensed in a single German phrase: *Geist aus der Maschine* — *Geist* (spirit, intellectual presence, mind in the Hegelian sense) *from the Machine*. It inverts the ancient *deus ex machina* (the external, artificial resolution dropped into a play) and refits it to the present situation: language now arrives with a *spirit-effect* whose origin is not the person at the desk but a statistical condensation of all prior writing. The seal answers the question the formula poses: in this work, *how much of the Geist is from the Machine?* An empty seal — the default — answers *none*. The dots count what was imported.

**The delegable mode.** This follows from the pragmatic level and is its consequence for where the border runs. The border between unmediated and machine-mediated writing does not run through the material of speech. It runs through its mode. Speech whose effect is settled before its wording — the mode Habermas calls success-oriented, and whose limit case Frankfurt describes as indifference toward what is said with full attention to its effect — requires no one who means anything. That is why it, and only it, can be handed to an apparatus without remainder. The substrate does not calculate: it pursues no interest and orients itself by no success. Calculation is a human mode, and its delegability follows from its emptiness, not from any capability of the machine. What is not delegable is the point at which someone stands behind a statement. The seal records the first and presupposes the second. This is also the anthropological ground of the refusal to detect: a procedure that machine-verifies whether a human was at work has already ceded the question to the mode it opposes.

## 4. The three sense dimensions

Following Luhmann, every sense-processing operates in three dimensions. The substrate alters each.

### Time *(Zeitdimension)*

Writing was historically a slow art. Brooding, incubation, layered revision over weeks. With the substrate, the genesis-time of text contracts toward zero, while reception-time remains unchanged. This produces a new asymmetry: rapid production, slow reading. The substrate itself carries a frozen past — its training corpus has a fixed cut-off, and what it mirrors is the written record up to that date. The author writes in the present, against an echo from a closed past.

**Implication for the registry:** the registry's construction is a deliberate counter-movement. Where the substrate accelerates production, the registry is built to be hard to lose — plain-text data, no database anyone must operate, no accounts anyone must maintain, licenses that let another party take it over. This is not a technical detail. It is a temporal stance.

The stance is stated as a construction, never as a term of years. An earlier formulation promised the registry for fifty years; that promise is withdrawn. No institution of one person can honour a term it will not outlive, and the architecture is checkable where the promise was only assertable. Duration is claimed by how the thing is built, not by how long it says it will last.

### Social *(Sozialdimension)*

Writing has always been socially structured: author and reader, author and editor, author and publisher. Each role had a position and a name. The substrate fits none of these. It represents an **aggregated anonymity** — the statistical condensation of all authors in its training corpus, none of them individually identifiable. The reader can no longer ask "who wrote this?" and receive a single answer.

**Implication for the registry:** Maschinenschrift is the institutional form that fills the social gap the substrate leaves. It does not restore the old categories of authorship. It creates a new one: the declared, catalogued mediation.

### Subject matter *(Sachdimension)*

The substrate knows linguistic patterns, not matters. It fills gaps by linguistic probability, not factual accuracy. This is why hallucination is not a bug but the structural consequence of pure language-modeling. The older form of this finding — that form can be delegated to the substrate and substance cannot — is ordered under §1 and now reads more precisely: what is not delegable is not a class of content but the commitment to it. Wording and content are both available from the substrate, in any degree; what never comes with them is anyone who answers for them.

**Implication for the registry:** the seal's three axes (W / G / B) are the direct structural answer to this asymmetry. They are not arbitrary categories. They follow from what the substrate is and is not — and their number follows from §1: what someone stands behind is not a further axis, because it cannot be imported.

## 5. The iterative writing process

Writing with the substrate displaces the iterative process from inside the author to a materialized exchange. Each iteration is now visible as text: prompt out, response in, judgment, modification, repeat. Three consequences follow.

**The prompt becomes a writing act.** The author must now articulate intent precisely enough to elicit a useful actualization. Vague prompts return vague text. Precision in instruction precedes precision in output.

**Authorial position clarifies against the average.** The substrate returns the statistically likely phrasing. The author's own voice becomes legible by *difference* from this average — by what is rejected, modified, sharpened against the returned text. Selection becomes a constitutive act of authorship. Selection is not only rejection of the average; it is also recognition of what the author already held, articulated through the iteration but not contributed by the substrate.

**The final text is a cut through a much larger field.** Before the substrate, a final text was one of a few drafts. Now it is one of potentially thousands of actualizations that the substrate could have produced and the author rejected, modified, or accepted. Authorship shifts from production toward curation.

The catalog does not document each iteration. It condenses the cumulative mediation into the two mediation axes, W and G.

## 6. Pain points addressed and not addressed

The substrate produces a landscape of concerns. Maschinenschrift addresses some and explicitly does not address others. This limitation is intentional and gives the institution its credibility.

**Addressed:**

- Authenticity *(Echtheit)* — through self-declaration
- Mediation *(Vermittlung)* — through the seal's two mediation axes, which make mediation legible
- Voice *(Stimme)* — indirectly, by making mediation visible and thereby making the unmediated also identifiable

**Not addressed (and not claimed to be):**

- Copyright of training data
- Concentration of linguistic infrastructure in few corporations
- Ecological footprint of model operation
- Cognitive atrophy from habitual delegation of formulation
- Truth and hallucination at the level of individual factual claims

The frontend does not promise more. Honesty about scope is part of the institution.

## 7. Seal axis semantics

The seal carries three axes. Their **typographic form** (square brackets, five slots, three rows) is unchanged. Their **semantic content** has passed through four states and thus three refinements: from the early triple (K/T/R) to (F/S/V), to (D/G/K) on 2026-05-24, and, as of 2026-08-02, to the present **(W/G/B) — Wortlaut, Geist, Beleg** *(glossed in English contexts as Wording, Geist, Record; the letters follow the German canon, as *Geist* always has — see §10 on Geist as untranslated loanword)*. The numeric values carry across unchanged: a D 4 declaration is a W 4 declaration.

The axes measure **substrate mediation** — what the machine contributed. The human is the unmarked default position: an empty seal `[ ]` means the work was produced by a human alone, without substrate intervention. Dots mark where the *Geist aus der Maschine* enters: the more dots, the more the spirit of this work was imported from outside the writing person. This polarity preserves the original Maschinenschrift visual convention and is consistent with §1's anti-anthropomorphism: the substrate is not *someone* whose presence we must positively detect; it is a *medium* whose traces we mark.

The earlier names *Form* and *Substanz/Substance* were retired because they described what the axis covered abstractly while the values measured something more specific — the linguistic surface (a matter of *diction*) and the contribution to the work's *spirit* (German: *Geist*). The earlier name *Verifikation/Verification* was retired because, once Maschinenschrift was established as register-keeper rather than assay office, *verification* falsely implied a checking authority. The B-axis names a *relation* (the entry corresponds to a bibliographically findable work), not a *verdict*.

The numerical scale (0–5) and the conversion from earlier declarations remain unchanged across every rename: an F=4 declaration became a D=4 declaration and is now a W=4 declaration; an S=2 declaration became and remains G=2. Only the axis labels change.

### Axis W — Wortlaut *(Wording)*

**Scale:** 0–5
**Measures:** degree of substrate contribution to the linguistic surface — idiomatic phrasing, stylistic register, syntactic flow, sentence-level formulation.

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | All formulation by the author; no substrate involvement at the linguistic surface |
| 1 | Minimal: typographical correction, isolated synonym suggestions |
| 2 | Pointwise: individual sentences stylistically refined through the substrate |
| 3 | Structural: paragraphs reformulated, transitions adjusted, larger passages stylistically mediated |
| 4 | Substantial: the majority of the final linguistic surface shaped through the substrate |
| 5 | Comprehensive: linguistic surface predominantly substrate-generated; author selected and integrated |

### Axis G — Geist *(Spirit, in the sense of esprit / Hegel's Geist — intellectual presence, not religious essence)*

**Scale:** 0–5
**Measures:** degree of substrate contribution to the work's spirit — ideas, arguments, structural decisions, factual material, content. This is the axis on which the contemporary AI debate centers: *Geist aus der Maschine* — how much of what appears as the work's mind was imported from outside the writing person?

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Idea, argument, content entirely by the author |
| 1 | Isolated factual lookups or terminological clarifications via substrate |
| 2 | Structural suggestions or alternative arguments prompted by substrate, selectively integrated |
| 3 | Substantive portions of argumentation or content development substrate-prompted |
| 4 | Major substance prompted by substrate and adopted with editorial selection |
| 5 | Substance largely substrate-generated; author functioned primarily as curator |

**Canonical reading of the axis name.** *Geist* sounds mentalistic but measures a question of provenance and vouching: how much of what the work holds out as its own came from a source that stands behind nothing. The axis is subject-indexed — for a private author the question reads *does this Geist mean it?*, for an institution *does this body bind itself to this?* — and what it counts is import from a source to which neither question can be put. Read this way the axis requires no access to anyone's inner states, and the standing objection against arguments from authorial intent does not reach it: the axis does not record what was meant, it records who vouches. The name is retained; this reading is canonical.

**What the scale cannot hold.** The five values count *how much* was imported, not *in what manner* the author stood in the exchange. Two works can carry the same G value and mean different things by it: one where the substance arrived as proposals and the author chose among them, one where the substance arrived the same way but was set in motion by the author's own positings and governed by rules the author had laid down. The axis has no slot for that difference. The `narration` field does, and this is one of the reasons it exists. Read the number as the measure of import and the narration as the account of what the author held against it. Declarants should not move a value up or down to compensate for something the narration is there to say.

**Genre conditionality.** The interpretation of Geist (and Wortlaut) is genre-conditional, and the condition is one of commitment, not of content class. The truth register changes the *object* of what the author stands behind, not the fact that they stand behind it: in fiction the author does not vouch for factual accuracy but for the text as a work; in non-fiction for both. The same value therefore reads differently in the two registers — where no claim on the world is made, imported substance touches nothing the author has undertaken to answer for; where one is made, it touches all of it. Readers should interpret W/G values in light of the declared `genre` field.

### Axis B — Beleg *(Record)*

**Type:** binary
**Measures:** whether the entry corresponds to a bibliographically findable work.

| State | Meaning | Notation |
|---|---|---|
| `belegt` (documented) | A bibliographic referent exists and has been confirmed | (no marker) |
| `ausstehend` (pending) | Self-declared; bibliographic referent not yet confirmed | † (dagger) |

The B-axis names a *relation* (between the declaration and the bibliographic world), not a *verdict*. It explicitly checks only one narrow correspondence: that the declared work is findable. The W and G values remain self-declarations whose truth is the author's responsibility.

**Admission is not attestation.** The division of labour above governs *whose statement* stands in the catalog. It is not touched by the plausibility threshold the catalog applies at the point of admission: before an entry is taken up, the declaration is checked for whether it coheres with the work it describes — whether it holds together in itself. That is a curatorial threshold for entry into a visibility layer, not an attestation of the values. Admission says the declaration does not fall apart; it does not say the values are right, and it never makes them the institution's statement. The procedure itself is published on the [submission page](/submit/) and is the governing form of it.

The naming carries a philosophical weight that aligns the seal with §1. The substrate operates *coherentially* — text-with-text, no access to a referent. The B-axis is the point at which Maschinenschrift restores a controlled, narrow correspondence claim — not for what the work *says*, but that the work *exists*.

**On the name.** Until Spec v7 this axis was called *Korrespondenz*, which stood in a known tension with the pragmatic ordering of §1, because accounts of truth as correspondence are the classical foil for pragmatist positions. The rename to *Beleg* removes the friction at the level of the word; the substantive point was always the same, and still holds: the B-axis makes no claim about the truth of what the work says, only that the work is bibliographically findable. It records a relation between declaration and world, not a verdict on content — which is why it sits inside a pragmatically ordered document without contradiction.

The reading above is the axis semantics, and it is narrow by design. It is to be kept apart from the wider sense the word *Korrespondenz* carries in the analytical layer, where it names the truth claim a text makes on the world — a different subject, and not the source of this axis.

The dagger is the pending marker, realized on two layers: on the seal glyph as a right-aligned half-stroke per the canonical seal specification ([Spec v7](/spec/siegel-v7/); the geometry has been unchanged since v5 — only the axis names were renamed, twice), and on the textual identification as the literal † character. Both layers carry the marker independently — readers who see only the glyph identify *ausstehend* by the half-stroke; readers who see only the identifier identify it by the dagger.

## 9. Data-layer directives

The schema below is the current, intentionally minimal canonical form. No timestamp register, no backend, no submission endpoints.

- **YAML primary store**: entries use `wortlaut:`, `geist:`, `beleg:` keys. The earlier `diktion:` / `korrespondenz:` keys, the `form:` / `substance:` / `verification:` keys, and the pre-Foundation `k_value` / `t_value` / `r_status` columns are all retired.
- **Validation constraints**: `wortlaut` and `geist` each accept integers 0–5; `beleg` accepts the strings `belegt` (documented) or `ausstehend` (pending).
- **Migration of existing values**: any entry declared under an earlier spec keeps its numerical values (F=4 → D=4 → W=4, S=2 → G=2). Field names migrate `form` → `diktion` → `wortlaut` and `verification` → `korrespondenz` → `beleg`; the `verification` value `verified` maps to `beleg: belegt`, `unverified` to `beleg: ausstehend`. Scale direction is preserved — only labels change. Entries still carrying an earlier key are normalized to the v7 schema at the rendering layer; no display path reads an axis key directly.
- **Field name language**: keys remain German across language versions of the site. They are identifiers, like *Maschinenschrift* and *Geist* themselves (see §10). The display strings are not translated either: the axis names stay German in both languages and carry an English gloss on first use (*wording*, *record*), and *Geist* is never replaced — §7 and the Spec are the governing form of this rule.
- **Dagger notation**: the `†` glyph is applied to the textual axis line at the rendering layer when `beleg: ausstehend`. It is not stored in data.
- **No genesis schema.** Fields for filing dates, publication dates, lead times, or manuscript states are not introduced. Authors who want cryptographic timestamping of manuscript states should use [OpenTimestamps](https://opentimestamps.org) independently of Maschinenschrift; the resulting `.ots` proofs remain with the author.
- **`declared_at`** remains the single temporal field — it records the day a work entered the catalog, derived from the Git commit history. It is not a priority anchor; the project does not make priority claims.

## 10. German terms in the English context

Two German words are not translated in English material — they enter the project's English vocabulary as proper terms, the way *Zeitgeist*, *Schadenfreude*, or *Bildung* have entered English.

### Maschinenschrift

The word *Maschinenschrift* is not translated. It remains German on the English site, in English documentation, and in English press materials. The German compound holds the entire relation in a single noun: the human writes, the machine mediates, the trace of both is in the script. English has no equivalent. *Typescript* names the artifact, not the practice. *Machine writing* is literal and misleading. The German word also takes its place in a tradition of compound nouns for institutional forms of writing — *Schrifttum*, *Schriftverkehr*, *Hochschrift* — that English approaches only through periphrasis.

Treated as a proper noun in English contexts, capitalized but not italicized after first use. First use in any English document should be set in italics with a brief gloss: *Maschinenschrift* (German: machine-script).

### Geist

The name of the G-axis is not translated either. *Geist* carries weights that English *spirit* loses (religious overload) and English *mind* loses (Hegel, Geisteswissenschaften, *esprit*-tradition). The project's diagnostic formula — *Geist aus der Maschine* (see §1) — is most precise in German, and the axis label should match the formula.

Treated like *Maschinenschrift*: first use in italics with brief gloss, *Geist* (German: spirit / intellectual presence; cf. Hegel's Geist, Geisteswissenschaften). After first use, plain noun. An occasional English gloss *Ghost from the Machine* may accompany the formula for readers familiar with the Ryle–Koestler–Shirow lineage.

## 11. Inaugural entry

The inaugural entry of the Maschinenschrift catalog is *La Scuola dei Silenzi* by I. O. Vargas (slug: `la-scuola-dei-silenzi`). This entry establishes the methodology in practice. Its declared values are **W=5 / G=3 / B=ausstehend** — comprehensive substrate contribution to the wording, substantive contribution to the spirit, bibliographic record pending.

The entry has been declared twice. The first declaration (2026-06-01) read *D=4 / G=2 / K=ausstehend* and was identical, in numbers, with the pre-Spec-v6 form *F=4 / S=2 / unverified*: through both renames the values carried across untouched. The values changed for the first time on 2026-08-02, when the finished work was re-assessed against its own process — W to 5, G to 3. A rename never moves a value; only a re-assessment does. Both events belong in the record, and the [catalog entry](/works/la-scuola-dei-silenzi/) names them.

Earlier drafts of this document anticipated a numbered registry with identifiers of the form `MS-YYYY-NNNN`. The current architecture — an open standard with a curated catalog rather than a centralized backend registry — addresses works by slug. The textual dagger notation defined in §7 and §9 is not dormant under this scheme: the catalog listing sets the † after the textual axis line of every entry declared `beleg: ausstehend`. It stands beside the glyph, not inside it, and is therefore untouched by the negative list of the seal specification, which forbids additional marks *on* the seal.

## Companion documents

This Foundation is the **linguistic** canon. Two companion documents complete the constitutional layer of Maschinenschrift: the **legal** canon (the CC BY 4.0 architecture, the trademark posture, the anti-capture rationale) and the **speech-act** canon, which establishes the declaration as a *Transparenz-Bekenntnis* — assertive with a commissive overtone, not third-party attestation — and the institution as register-keeper rather than assay office. Both are working documents and are not published.

The three are disjoint truth-layers; they remain consistent with each other. **Precedence**, where they touch (notably the B-axis in §7 above): each layer governs its own subject matter — this Foundation the language and the axis semantics, the licensing strategy the legal construction, the self-declaration paper the speech-act classification and the role of the institution. Outside its own subject a layer describes; it does not decide. Where two layers decide the same question, the most recent constitutional clarification governs the reading. A derivation that a later clarification has withdrawn does not survive in the layer that first carried it.
